# Compliance and ethics

This is practical guidance, not legal advice. When a question has real legal or tax consequences, say so and point the user to a professional or the official source.

## Disclosure of AI generation

- EU AI Act Article 50: its transparency duties have applied since 2 August 2026. Deployers who publish AI-generated or manipulated image, audio or video that resembles real people, places or events must disclose that it is artificial. The Digital Omnibus did not delay these deployer duties; the only transition concerns machine-readable marking by providers of systems already on the market (until 2 December 2026). Sources: European Commission Article 50 FAQ; regulation-ai.eu (August 2026).
- Practical implementation: bio line ("AI-generated character · real places"), disclosure line in every caption, the platform's own AI label where available, and a plain statement in the product's small print. Keep screenshots of the bio and a sample of labelled posts as evidence.
- Outside the EU, platform rules still require labelling of realistic synthetic media; check the platform's current policy at setup.

## Likeness and identity

- The persona's face, name and voice must be original. No celebrity or real-person likeness, no cloned voice without written permission from the voice owner, no names that impersonate a real creator.
- Never present the persona as a minor, never sexualise the persona, and keep styling modest; this protects the account and the product.
- Do not claim professional credentials the persona cannot have (doctor, physiotherapist, financial adviser, therapist).

## Licensing (what the license gate enforces)

- Every published asset must trace to tools whose terms allow commercial use of outputs, with no visible watermark. Free plans are often non-commercial.
- Open models: check both the code license and the weights license, and regional exclusions.
- Background plates: the user's own photos, or photos licensed for commercial use. Not Google Maps, Earth or Street View imagery; not tourism-board banks licensed only for promoting a country; not random search results.
- Music: add licensed audio in-app from the platform library, or royalty-free tracks with a commercial social license. Never bake a copyrighted song into the file.

## Places and people in the background

- Filming in public places is generally fine; private businesses, marinas, markets, saunas and some nature reserves may need permission for commercial content.
- Identifiable strangers in commercial content raise privacy issues (GDPR in the EU). Keep background people small, soft and unidentifiable, or blur them.
- Never show readable signs with real business names as if they endorse the persona.

## Claims

- Health: habits, routines and traditions only; no cures, treatments, diagnoses or dosages; add "not medical advice" where a routine touches the body.
- Money: principles and habits only; no guaranteed returns, no specific investment instructions.
- Every fact in a hook, caption or product page must be true and sourced; hedge where evidence is thin.

## Advertising and sponsorships

- Paid partnerships and gifted products must be marked as advertising using the platform's paid-partnership tool and the local legal requirement (EU consumer law treats hidden advertising as an unfair practice).
- The persona's own product can be promoted freely, but prices, contents and refund terms must be accurate.

## The product's small print (template)

"[Name] is an AI-generated character. The routines in this guide are ordinary habits, not medical, financial or psychological advice. If something hurts, stop and ask a professional. If you are struggling, talk to someone: [real, local, verified helplines with hours]. Personal use only; please do not resell or redistribute."

Verify helpline numbers and opening hours at build time and cite the official source.
